A Layman's Guide to Exporting Services from India (FEMA 2026)

A plain-English guide to the new EDF (Export Declaration Form) under FEMA, effective 1 October 2026 — who files it, when, and how to stay compliant.

Published on 5 October 2026 · Category: FEMA

If you earn money from clients outside India, the rules of the game changed on 1 October 2026. The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) replaced the old export rulebook with a new one under the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 (FEMA), and introduced a single, simple form — the EDF, or Export Declaration Form — for reporting your exports. Here is everything you need to know in plain language.

The Big Picture: What Is This All About?

Whenever you earn from outside India, the government wants to know three things: what you are exporting, how much it is worth, and whether the money actually came back to India. These rules come under FEMA, 1999, which is administered by the RBI. In simple words, FEMA is the rulebook for "money crossing India's borders."

In January 2026, the RBI replaced the old export rules with a brand-new set that officially took effect on 1 October 2026. The centrepiece of the new rulebook is a single declaration called the EDF (Export Declaration Form).

First, Are You Even an "Exporter"?

Before worrying about forms, ask yourself: did I actually sell something to a specific person outside India? Under FEMA, "export of service" means the provision of services from India to a person outside India. Notice the key word — a person. You need an identifiable customer outside India who received your service and paid you for it.

The easy examples (these are exports):

  • A company in the USA pays you to design a logo.
  • A client in Dubai hires you for consulting.
  • A firm in London buys your software.
  • A German agency pays you for coding work.

The tricky example (this is not a simple export): you post videos on YouTube and earn ad money, or post reels on Instagram and earn platform money. There is no customer — YouTube is a platform, not your client. You did not send an invoice to anyone, and the money is a share of advertising revenue based on views.

What Is the EDF (Export Declaration Form)?

The EDF is a declaration you give to your bank saying: "I sold services worth this much to this foreign client, and I promise to bring that money back into India." It is the paperwork that connects your sale to the money coming back. It is not a tax return and is not related to GST — it is a foreign-exchange declaration under FEMA.

Earlier, software exporters used a separate form called SOFTEX. From 1 October 2026, SOFTEX is gone, and software is also reported through the EDF. Now there is one form for everything: goods, services, and software.

Who Must File the EDF?

Any exporter of services, irrespective of the value of the export. This includes freelancers (designers, writers, developers, consultants), IT and software companies, marketing and design agencies, professionals providing services abroad — and even creators and YouTubers, but only if what they do genuinely qualifies as an export. For example, if a foreign brand pays you directly for sponsored content, that is an export and needs an EDF.

Where Do You File the EDF?

Your situation Where to file
Non-software services (consulting, design, marketing, etc.) Your bank (called the "Authorised Dealer" or AD)
Software exports Your bank or STPI
If you're in an SEZ (Special Economic Zone) SEZ Development Commissioner

In short, for a normal businessman or proprietor outside an SEZ, you file with your own bank.

When Do You File the EDF?

The general rule is to file within 30 days from the end of the month in which you raised the invoice. There is also good news on timing:

  • One form for the whole month: you can file one EDF covering all your clients and invoices for the entire month — no separate form per invoice.
  • Payment-date relief (non-software services only): you can file on or before the date you receive payment instead of the 30-day deadline.
  • Extensions: if you are late for a genuine reason, your bank can give you more time if you ask and explain.

What Information Goes in the EDF?

The form has two parts:

  • Part A — General information: your name, address, PAN, GSTIN, and IEC (Import Export Code); your bank's AD code and details; the type of export (goods or service); the client's details and country; a description of the service; and the total value.
  • Part B — Details of each service export: client name and address, country, invoice number and date, currency and amount, net realisable value, contract number (if any), and the description of service with its SAC code (the code for your service type under GST).

Step by Step: From Sale to Money in the Bank

  1. You sign a client outside India.
  2. You deliver the service and raise an invoice.
  3. You prepare the EDF, one per month, covering all invoices.
  4. You submit the EDF to your bank.
  5. Your bank enters it into EDPMS (the government's export monitoring system) within 5 working days — you do not report it yourself.
  6. The client pays you in foreign currency.
  7. The money lands in your bank account.
  8. Your bank marks the file "closed" in EDPMS once the money is received.

Importantly, you do not upload the EDF to any RBI website yourself. Your bank does the system entry; you just give the form to the bank.

Registrations You Need First

To export services smoothly as a proprietor, get these in place first:

  • PAN — your basic tax ID (Income Tax).
  • IEC (Import Export Code) — mandatory for any export (DGFT).
  • GST registration — mandatory for export (GST portal).
  • AD Code registration — links your bank to your export identity (your bank).
  • LUT (Letter of Undertaking) — to export without paying GST upfront (GST portal).
  • STPI registration — only if you export software (STPI).
  • Udyam / MSME registration (optional) — for benefits and schemes (Udyam portal).

Getting Your Money Back: The Realisation Timeline

Once you invoice a client, you must bring the full export value back to India within a specified period. From 1 October 2026, the timelines are:

  • Export of goods: 9 months from the date of shipment.
  • Export of services: 9 months from the date of invoice.
  • Goods exported to an overseas warehouse: 9 months from the date of sale from the warehouse.
  • Export invoiced or settled in Indian Rupees: 12 months.
  • Project exports: as per the contractual payment terms.

What Happens If You Don't File?

There is no automatic penalty per form, but the consequences are real. Without an EDF, your bank cannot reconcile your money, because there is no file in the system to match your incoming payment — and the bank may hold up crediting your foreign money. Your invoice may show as "unrealised", and money not reconciled within 9 months makes you look like a non-compliant exporter.

Breaking FEMA rules can attract a penalty of up to 3 times the amount involved (or ₹2 lakh where the amount is not quantifiable), plus daily penalties for continuing. Repeated non-compliance can also get you placed on the Caution List, after which you cannot export without advance payment or a letter of credit. The simple advice is to just file it on time — it is one form a month, and it protects you.

Quick FAQs

  • Is EDF mandatory from 1 October 2026? Yes, for anyone exporting services.
  • Can one EDF cover multiple clients or invoices? Yes, one EDF can cover the whole month's exports.
  • Do I upload the EDF on RBI's website myself? No — you give it to your bank, and the bank enters it in the system.
  • Is a small invoice exempt? No, the EDF must be submitted for every export under FEMA regardless of value.
  • Does YouTube or Instagram income need an EDF? Not if it is pure ad-revenue sharing with no direct client. But a direct payment from a foreign brand for sponsored content is an export and needs an EDF.
  • Is the EDF the same as a GST return? No — the EDF is a FEMA / foreign-exchange declaration, while GST is a separate, additional compliance.

Your Monthly Cheat-Sheet

  1. Confirm the sale is a genuine "export of service" under FEMA.
  2. Note the client's name, country, invoice number, date, currency, and amount.
  3. Note the service description and SAC code.
  4. Prepare one EDF for the month.
  5. File it with your bank within 30 days of month-end.
  6. Keep proof of submission.
  7. Confirm with your bank that they entered it in EDPMS.
  8. Follow up on the payment (realise within 9 months).

A Note on Export of Goods

If you physically export goods from India, the EDF compliance is integrated with the Customs process. For exports through an EDI port, the EDF is deemed to have been submitted as part of the Shipping Bill, so there is no separate EDF to submit to the bank. For exports through a non-EDI port, the EDF is furnished separately and the Authorised Dealer enters the details in EDPMS within five working days of receiving it. The export value must be declared in full, and proceeds must ordinarily be realised and repatriated within 9 months from the date of shipment (12 months where invoiced or settled in INR).

From 1 October 2026, exporting services means one new habit: every month, file one EDF with your bank. Keep clean invoices, chase your payments within 9 months, and keep the EDF separate from your GST in your mind. Do this and you stay completely compliant, with no surprises.
